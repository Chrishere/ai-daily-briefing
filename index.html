We call the collection of these patterns the J-space—named after the technique we used to find them, involving a mathematical concept called the Jacobian. The J-space wasn't designed or programmed by us, but instead emerged on its own during Claude's training process. Claude uses its J-space for internal reasoning. If you ask Claude to solve a problem that requires multiple steps, the intermediate steps will light up in its J-space, even when it doesn't say them out loud.
